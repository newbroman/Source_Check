    What the colours mean

    Red — the website or channel is named in a published investigation or sanctions decision as Russian state media or part of a Russian influence operation.

    Amber — not on a list, but the address imitates a well-known outlet (a trick used by the Doppelganger campaign and others), or a channel uses a state outlet's name.

    Orange — not a documented Russian operation, but independent researchers give the site a low reliability score (under 40 out of 100).

    Green — not on any list. That does not mean the story is accurate; it only means the source isn't a documented Russian operation.

    Reliability score — shown where available: a 0–100 rating for about 11,500 news sites, combining six independent rating sets (Lin et al., 2023, PNAS Nexus). Under 40 is low, 40–59 mixed, 60 and over generally reliable. It rates the publisher's track record, not the political leaning, and not the individual story. The ratings date from 2023.

    Grey — the link hides where it really goes (Google News, link shorteners, Facebook posts). Open it, then share the article itself.

    This app judges the source, not the content. Plenty of people share similar views without any connection to a state operation. Everything is checked on your phone; nothing you share is sent anywhere, except a YouTube video address, which is sent to YouTube to look up the channel name.
